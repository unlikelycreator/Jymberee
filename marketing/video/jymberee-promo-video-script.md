# Jymberee – Promotional Video Script (Vertical 9:16, Google Flow, 3 × ~10 s)

**Title:** "Help, in seconds"
**Length:** about 30 seconds (3 clips of about 10 seconds each)
**Format:** **vertical 9:16, 1080 × 1920**, for Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts, WhatsApp Status and Facebook Stories
**Tone:** tense to hopeful. Real people, real street, no cartoon or 3D look.
**Story in one line:** A young woman walking home alone at night feels unsafe. She taps the Jymberee SOS button. Her family and people nearby are alerted at once, and help reaches her in seconds.

## 2. Vertical framing rules

- **Keep the action in the centre.** Faces, hands and the phone stay inside the middle 70% of the frame.
- **Safe zones.** Leave the top ~12% and bottom ~18% free of anything important, because Reels and Shorts put the caption, buttons and profile name there.
- **Shot sizes.** Use close-ups and medium shots (head to waist) more than wide shots. Wide moments are filled by height (tilts and crane-ups), not width.
- **Camera moves.** Use push-ins, vertical tilts, crane-ups and rack focus. Avoid fast side-to-side pans.
- **Depth over width.** Put background characters behind or above the subject in the frame (on the far side of the street, further up the frame), not beside her.
- **Logo space.** The last frame of clip 3 leaves the upper third empty for the logo.

## 3. Continuity rules (keep these identical in all 3 prompts)

- **Character (Priya):** Indian woman, about 27, shoulder-length straight black hair, light-brown skin, small gold stud earrings. She wears a mustard-yellow kurta, dark-blue jeans and a grey cross-body bag, and carries a black smartphone in her right hand.
- **Location:** a narrow residential street in Mumbai at night, wet from rain. Warm orange sodium streetlights, a small lit corner grocery shop, parked scooters, a few apartment windows lit.
- **Lighting:** night, warm orange streetlight on her face, cool blue shadows, light reflections on the wet road.
- **Camera look:** vertical 9:16, handheld cinematic, 35 mm lens, shallow depth of field, natural film grain, true-to-life colours.
- **Narrator voice:** the same warm, calm, clear female narrator in her early 30s, with a neutral Indian-English accent and a slow, confident pace. It's voice-over only; nobody on screen speaks.
- **Phone screen:** show only a simple large glowing red circle (the SOS button) on a dark screen. There is no readable text, no app UI details and no other icons.

## 4. Google Flow prompts (copy and paste)

> **Before you start:** set the aspect ratio in Flow to **9:16 (portrait)** for every clip.
> **How to keep the clips joined:** generate Clip 1 first. For Clip 2, use **Extend** on Clip 1 in Flow (or **Frames to Video** with Clip 1's last frame as the starting frame). Then do the same from Clip 2 to Clip 3. Paste each prompt exactly as written. They repeat the same character, place and voice so the model keeps everything consistent.

## 5. Tips for a clean result

1. **Aspect ratio.** Check that Flow is set to 9:16 before each generation. If a clip comes out horizontal, regenerate it rather than cropping, because cropping loses quality and cuts off faces.
2. **Clip length.** Flow (Veo) clips are often about 8 seconds. If yours come out at 8 s, the voice-over lines still fit, because each is under 7 seconds when spoken slowly.
3. **Generate 2–4 versions of each clip** and pick the one with the cleanest hands, phone screen and voice.
4. **Voice consistency.** The AI voice can sound slightly different from clip to clip. If it does, mute the generated voice in your editor and record or generate the three narrator lines in one session (one voice, one take) and lay them over the clips. Keep the generated background sound.
5. **Phone screen.** If the screen still glitches, replace it in the editor with a real screenshot of the Jymberee SOS screen. Track it onto the phone, or cut to a clean full-screen vertical insert of the app for 1–2 seconds, which suits the vertical format well.
6. **Name pronunciation.** If "Jymberee" is mispronounced, put the name only in your own recorded voice-over, or show it as text on the end card.
7. **Text and captions.** Add the logo, the tagline, the store badges and any captions in the editor (CapCut, Premiere, DaVinci). Keep captions in the middle of the screen, above the bottom safe zone. Burned-in captions help, since many people watch Reels with the sound off.
8. **Joining the clips.** Use a straight cut or a short 6–8 frame cross-dissolve between clips. Add one music track across all 30 seconds so the audio feels continuous.
9. **Export.** 1080 × 1920, 30 fps, H.264, about 10–15 Mbps.
10. **Claims.** The video shows only features the app has today: the SOS button, a WhatsApp alert with location to family, and push alerts to nearby users. Avoid showing earbud or shake triggers until they're confirmed working on both Android and iOS.
