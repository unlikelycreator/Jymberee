# Jymberee – Promotional Video Script (Vertical 9:16, Google Flow, 3 × ~10 s)

**Title:** "Any emergency. One tap."
**Length:** about 38 seconds (3 story clips of about 10 seconds each, plus an 8-second end screen)
**Format:** **vertical 9:16, 1080 × 1920**, for Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts, WhatsApp Status and Facebook Stories
**Tone:** serious and urgent, then reassuring, in the style of a professional public-safety awareness ad.
**Story in one line:** Emergencies can strike anyone: a medical emergency, a fire, a road accident, a break-in. In every case one tap on Jymberee alerts loved ones and people nearby, and help arrives in seconds.

---

## The "thin line": how these prompts stay serious and pass Flow's safety filters

Flow blocks prompts that describe harm happening (impacts, attacks, bodies, intruders). It allows the **moment right after**, as long as the danger is clearly shown through **reactions, consequences and atmosphere**. Every prompt below follows these rules:

| Do (allowed, still feels serious) | Don't (gets blocked) |
|---|---|
| Show the **aftermath**: a scooter lying on its side, a car stopped with hazard lights | Show the impact: "car hits / strikes / crashes into" |
| Show **reactions**: panic, a worried face, someone running, a hand shaking | Show the victim's body: "unresponsive", "lying still", "collapses", "injured" |
| Show **danger through atmosphere**: heavy smoke, orange glow, a flashing alarm, a broken window | Show destruction or harm: "burning", "fire engulfs", "shatters", "attack" |
| Show **implied threats**: an open front door, scattered drawers, a torch beam outside | Show the criminal: "masked intruder", "burglar", "thief", "weapon" |
| Use **neutral medical words**: "feels unwell", "sits down heavily", "short of breath" | Use clinical alarm words: "cardiac arrest", "heart attack", "collapse" |
| Use "public-safety awareness advertisement" framing | Use "violent", "harmful", "intense", "disturbing" |

The **voice-over** carries the seriousness ("A medical emergency. A fire. An accident. A break-in."), so the pictures don't have to show harm.

## 3. Vertical framing rules

- **Keep the action in the centre.** Faces, hands and the phone stay inside the middle 70% of the frame.
- **Safe zones.** Leave the top ~12% and bottom ~18% free of anything important, because Reels and Shorts put the caption, buttons and profile name there.
- **Shot sizes.** Use close-ups and medium shots more than wide shots.
- **Camera moves.** Use push-ins, vertical tilts, crane-ups and rack focus. Avoid fast side-to-side pans.
- **Logo space.** The last frame of clip 3 leaves the upper third empty for the logo.

## 5. If a prompt is still blocked

1. **Generate one shot at a time.** A single sensitive phrase blocks the whole montage. Paste only one SHOT section, plus the first paragraph and the CAMERA, AUDIO, QUALITY and AVOID sections. That way you can see exactly which shot triggers the filter. Then trim each result to 2–3 seconds in Scenebuilder. This also gives cleaner, more serious-looking shots.
2. **Soften the triggering shot one step further:**
   - **Medical:** "Mr. Rao feels dizzy and sits down holding his chest; Ananya rushes to him worried."

## 6. Tips for a clean result

1. **Use Ingredients.** Character reference images keep Mr. Rao, Ananya and the others looking the same in clips 1, 2 and 3.
2. **Aspect ratio.** Check that Flow is set to 9:16 before each generation. If a clip comes out horizontal, regenerate it rather than cropping.
3. **Clip length.** Flow (Veo) clips are often about 8 seconds. If yours come out at 8 s, the voice-over lines still fit, because each is under 7 seconds when spoken slowly.
4. **Generate 2–4 versions of each clip** and pick the one with the cleanest hands, phone screens and voice.
5. **Voice consistency.** With a montage, the most reliable approach is to mute the AI voice and record or generate the three narrator lines in one session (one voice, one take), then lay them over the clips. Keep the generated sound effects, and use one music track across all 30 seconds.
6. **Phone screens.** If a screen glitches, replace it in the editor with a real screenshot of the Jymberee SOS screen, or cut to a clean full-screen vertical insert of the app for 1–2 seconds.
7. **Name pronunciation.** If "Jymberee" is mispronounced, put the name only in your own recorded voice-over, or show it as text on the end card.
8. **Text and captions.** Add the logo, the tagline, the store badges and the captions in the editor. Optional on-screen labels during clip 1 match the app's emergency categories: "Medical", "Fire", "Accident", "Theft". Keep them in the middle of the screen, above the bottom safe zone.
9. **Export.** 1080 × 1920, 30 fps, H.264, about 10–15 Mbps.
10. **Claims.** The video shows only features the app has today: the SOS button, a WhatsApp alert with live location to loved ones, and push alerts to nearby users.
